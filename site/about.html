About the data

Morocco's Haut-Commissariat au Plan (HCP) publishes census results by commune, but each census uses its own codes, its own commune list and, before 2015, a different regional map. This project puts the 2004, 2014 and 2024 censuses (RGPH) on a common commune key, so the same place can be followed across twenty years, and adds approximate commune shapes so the data can be mapped.

What is here

  • Per-census tables in HCP's own units: 1,689 communes in 2004 (16-region map), 1,538 in 2014, 1,503 in 2024.
  • A crosswalk linking the 2014 commune list to the 2004 and 2024 lists. All 1,538 communes are linked to 2024, and 1,528 to the 2004 commune profiles. The 10 without a 2004 link were carved out of a larger commune after 2004.
  • A three-census panel of HCP's poverty and development indices (2004 IDH, IDS and monetary poverty; multidimensional poverty for 2004, 2014 and 2024). Where a commune did not exist in an earlier census, its value is imputed from the same cercle (population-weighted) and flagged in src04/src14/src24.
  • Approximate geometry: Thiessen cells and seed points, with queen-contiguity weights.

The map

The map shows the 50 indicators observed in all three censuses, so that change can be read from one census to the next. The other indicators, observed in one or two censuses, are in the downloads and listed in the catalogue.

The map can be read in English, French or Arabic. In French and Arabic, commune and province names are HCP's own, from its 2024 legal population list, and indicator labels are HCP's own wording; the catalogue gives the source of each. Where HCP publishes no Arabic label for an indicator, its French label is shown. Definitions, notes and the other pages are in English only; help from fluent French or Arabic speakers to translate them is welcome.

How it was built

Everything is rebuilt from HCP's published files by the code in the repository: uv run mc fetch downloads the raw files and checks them against data/raw/manifest.json, and uv run mc all rebuilds every table, the geometry and this site. The 2004 commune profiles come from HCP's Maroc en Chiffres application (mc crawl-2004); the 2004 indices from Annexe 2 of HCP's 2004 poverty volume; the 2014 and 2024 indicators from HCP's commune-level workbooks, with the 2024 tables on commuting, migration, the urban housing stock, economic establishments and douars; multidimensional poverty from HCP's 2004–2014 poverty map and its May-2025 database. Full provenance, with file checksums, is in the repository's docs/.

Commune shapes

HCP does not distribute commune boundaries as a dataset, and none were provided on request. Its RGPH 2024 results platform does draw them, and the map offers them as an optional layer (HCP 2024 boundaries). Their licence for reuse as data is not stated, so the default map and the downloads use approximations built only from openly licensed inputs. Each commune gets one seed point from the GeoNames gazetteer, or from Wikidata where GeoNames lacks the commune, and the country is divided into the cells nearest each point. Where both place a commune more than 25 km apart, the choice between them is recorded in catalog/seed_review.csv. The cells show roughly where a commune is, not its true extent or area; do not compute densities from them. The outline is Natural Earth. Every geometry input is openly licensed.

HCP's 2024 layer is keyed by 2014 commune codes, so every 2014 commune has its polygon and 2024 values follow through the crosswalk. 2004 values are drawn on these later boundaries too. The 2004 profiles report a rural commune apart from its autonomous centres (its small towns); since 2014 HCP counts them together, so a 2014 commune's 2004 value combines them, population-weighted. Communes merged after 2004 are combined the same way, and renames are followed; each such decision is recorded, with its population and location evidence, in catalog/link_review.csv. The 10 communes with no 2004 link have no 2004 value.

Known gaps

  • One commune (Sidi Mohamed Ben Mansour, Kénitra) has no seed point yet and no Thiessen cell (points_unmatched.csv); it appears with the HCP 2024 boundaries layer, and its data are in the tables. Five others are placed from gazetteer spellings recorded in catalog/seed_names.csv.
  • In 2014 the six largest cities are split into 41 arrondissements; in 2024 they are single communes. The map merges the arrondissements into one city cell (sums for counts, population-weighted means for rates); the tables keep them separate.
  • 10 communes created after 2004 from part of another commune (Had Soualem, Soualem Trifiya, Oulad Ziyane, Ameur Chamalia, Sidi Mohamed Ben Mansour and five more) have no 2004 value, and three 2004 communes (Ain Dorbane, Lakhiaita, Soualem) are not linked, since splitting their values would need assumptions.
  • Only both-sexes, both-milieux values are published so far; HCP's breakdowns by sex and by urban/rural milieu are not yet extracted.
  • Some indicators change definition between censuses (activity rate, urban status); the catalogue flags them. The poverty-map MPI (2004, 2014) and the 2025 MPI database (2014, 2024) use different methods and scales; do not join the two series.
  • Western Sahara: RGPH 2024 covers the communes of the Laâyoune-Sakia El Hamra and Dakhla-Oued Ed-Dahab regions and they are included as HCP reports them. Inclusion follows the source statistics and implies no position on the territory's status.
