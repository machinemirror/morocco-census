About the data

Morocco's Haut-Commissariat au Plan (HCP) publishes census results by commune, but each census uses its own codes, its own commune list and, before 2015, a different regional map. This project puts the 2004, 2014 and 2024 censuses (RGPH) on a common commune key, so the same place can be followed across twenty years, and maps it on HCP's own commune boundaries.

What is here

  • Per-census tables in HCP's own units: 1,689 communes in 2004 (16-region map), 1,538 in 2014, 1,503 in 2024.
  • A crosswalk linking the 2014 commune list to the 2004 and 2024 lists. All 1,538 communes are linked to 2024, and 1,537 to the 2004 commune profiles.
  • Urban/rural and male/female breakdowns of the commune tables: HCP's milieu sheets and sex blocks for 2014 and 2024; for 2004, the profiles' female counts, and the profiles' own split of each rural commune from its urban centres.
  • A three-census panel of HCP's poverty and development indices (2004 IDH, IDS and monetary poverty; multidimensional poverty for 2004, 2014 and 2024). Where a commune did not exist in an earlier census, its value is imputed from the same cercle (population-weighted) and flagged in src04/src14/src24.
  • Geometry: HCP's commune boundaries (the communes of 2014 and 2024), and one point per commune from openly licensed gazetteers, each with queen-contiguity weights.

The map

The map shows the 50 indicators observed in all three censuses, so that change can be read from one census to the next. The other indicators, observed in one or two censuses, are in the downloads and listed in the catalogue. Population switches the map to the urban or rural part of each commune (49 indicators) or to men or women (25: age, marital status, education, youth illiteracy, status in employment); a breakdown is offered only where all three censuses have it. Households have no sex breakdown.

Colours: each indicator's classes are set from its values in all three censuses together, so a colour means the same value in 2004 and 2024. The breaks are whichever of quantiles, natural breaks (Fisher-Jenks) or natural breaks on a log scale fit the indicator's distribution best, with no class holding under 0.5% of values; where a tenth of values or more are 0 (a language not spoken, no apartments), 0 has a class of its own. Change maps are symmetric around 0, with breaks at the 20th, 50th and 80th percentiles of the size of the change.

2004 urban and rural values come from the 2004 profiles themselves, which report each rural commune apart from its autonomous centres: municipalities, arrondissements and centres count as urban. They hold 16.3 million people, 99.2% of HCP's 2004 urban total. 2004 male and female rates are computed from the female counts the profiles give under each category; youth illiteracy is for ages 15 to 24, as HCP published it in 2004.

The map can be read in English, French or Arabic. In French and Arabic, commune and province names are HCP's own, from its 2024 legal population list, and indicator labels are HCP's own wording; the catalogue gives the source of each. Where HCP publishes no Arabic label for an indicator, its French label is shown. Definitions, notes and the other pages are in English only; help from fluent French or Arabic speakers to translate them is welcome.

How it was built

Everything is rebuilt from HCP's published files by the code in the repository: uv run mc fetch downloads the raw files and checks them against data/raw/manifest.json, and uv run mc all rebuilds every table, the geometry and this site. The 2004 commune profiles come from HCP's Maroc en Chiffres application (mc crawl-2004); the 2004 indices from Annexe 2 of HCP's 2004 poverty volume; the 2014 and 2024 indicators from HCP's commune-level workbooks, with the 2024 tables on commuting, migration, the urban housing stock, economic establishments and douars; multidimensional poverty from HCP's 2004–2014 poverty map and its May-2025 database; commune boundaries, and French and Arabic names and labels, from HCP's 2024 legal population list and RGPH 2024 results platform. Full provenance, with file checksums, is in the repository's docs/PROVENANCE.md.

The pipeline, tests, this site and the documentation were developed with AI coding assistants (Anthropic's Claude Code and OpenAI's Codex) under the author's direction. The statistics are HCP's; each linkage decision is recorded with its evidence in the repository, and every published number is regenerated from HCP's files and checked by the tests, so the results can be audited without relying on how the code was written.

Commune shapes

The map draws HCP's own commune boundaries. They are served by HCP's RGPH 2024 results platform, but every polygon carries its 2014 commune code and the urban centres of rural communes are drawn as the 2014 census delimited them, so they appear to be HCP's 2014 census cartography, reused; HCP does not say whether any line was redrawn for 2024. The communes themselves are the same in both censuses: the 1,497 communes and municipalities of 2014 match those of 2024 one to one, and the 41 arrondissements of 2014 are the 6 city communes of 2024. The boundaries are in the downloads with queen-contiguity weights, and should be credited to HCP. HCP publishes them per province, and their edges leave hairline gaps along province borders, so the weights allow 50 m of tolerance.

Each commune also has one point, from the GeoNames gazetteer or from Wikidata where GeoNames lacks it, published for uses that need locations rather than polygons. Where the two place a commune more than 25 km apart, the choice between them is recorded in catalog/seed_review.csv. Until release 2026.9.6 the map's alternative layer was a set of Thiessen cells built around these points; HCP's boundaries replace it.

HCP's layer is keyed by 2014 commune codes, so every 2014 commune has its polygon and 2024 values follow through the crosswalk. 2004 values are drawn on these later boundaries too. The 2004 profiles report a rural commune apart from its autonomous centres (its small towns); since 2014 HCP counts them together, so a 2014 commune's 2004 value combines them, population-weighted. Communes merged after 2004 are combined the same way, and renames are followed; each such decision is recorded, with its population and location evidence, in catalog/link_review.csv. Where a commune was split after 2004, each part carries the 2004 rates of the commune it came from, and 2004 counts are shared between the parts. The shares come from HCP's own 2004 populations on the 2014 boundaries, which its regional offices publish for some provinces (Berrechid, Nouaceur, Settat, Kénitra, Sidi Slimane, Salé, M'Diq-Fnideq); elsewhere from the parts' 2014 population. The decrees that created the communes in 2008-2009 list them but do not say what they were carved from.

Known gaps

  • One commune (Sidi Mohamed Ben Mansour, Kénitra) has no gazetteer point (points_unmatched.csv); it has its HCP boundary. Five others are placed from gazetteer spellings recorded in catalog/seed_names.csv.
  • In 2014 the six largest cities are split into 41 arrondissements; in 2024 they are single communes. The map merges the arrondissements into one city (sums for counts, population-weighted means for rates); the tables keep them separate, and the crosswalk's code24_commune and unit give each arrondissement its city's 2024 commune and map unit.
  • Ait Ali ou Lahcen (Khémisset), created after 2004, has no 2004 value: nothing published shows which commune it came from. In Berrechid, HCP's figures show Had Soualem, Soualem Trifiya and Sahel Oulad H'Riz gained territory from Lakhiaita and parts of Sidi Rahal Chatai and Sidi El Mekki, but not which piece went where; their 2004 values cover only the part that is known, and validation.json lists the gaps.
  • The breakdowns cover the indicators workbooks; the other 2024 workbooks (commuting, migration, urban housing, establishments, douars) and the poverty indices are published for the whole commune only.
  • Some indicators change definition between censuses (activity rate, urban status); the catalogue flags them. The poverty-map MPI (2004, 2014) and the 2025 MPI database (2014, 2024) use different methods and scales; do not join the two series.
  • Western Sahara: RGPH 2024 covers the communes of the Laâyoune-Sakia El Hamra and Dakhla-Oued Ed-Dahab regions and they are included as HCP reports them. Inclusion follows the source statistics and implies no position on the territory's status.
